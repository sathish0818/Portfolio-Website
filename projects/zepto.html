UX CASE STUDY

Zepto's "Place Order" button doesn't wait to place the order.

A live walkthrough of Zepto's Cash / UPI-on-delivery checkout, and a small, deliberate fix that puts the tap back where it belongs: with the user.

Product
Zepto
Type
UX Audit + Redesign
Year
2026

THE PROBLEM

The interface had already decided for me.

On a routine grocery order, I picked "Pay Cash/UPI on delivery", expecting the same kind of confirmation "Pay Online" gives. Instead, the checkout screen appeared with a "Place Order Now" button that was already filling itself in, like a countdown I hadn't started.

By the time I'd read the amount, the screen was gone and the order was placed. There was no moment to reconsider the item, check the address, or simply catch up with what had just happened. The interface had already decided for me.

~1.4sfrom the checkout screen appearing to the order being placed
0taps required to confirm the purchase
1 of 2payment paths on checkout behave this way

WHO PAYS FOR THIS

The user and the business both lose.

For the user

Say you're mid-cart, planning to add three more items before checking out, but a stray tap lands on "Place Order Now" before you meant it to. On this flow, that's it. There's no pause to catch the mistake, no chance to add what you actually came for. The order that gets delivered isn't the one you wanted, and the next time you order from Zepto, you second-guess every tap.

For the business

That same accidental order isn't free for Zepto either. A delivery partner gets assigned, a dark store picks and packs it, a vehicle is dispatched, all for an order the customer didn't actually mean to place. Multiply this by thousands of daily orders, and a two-second confirmation step is far cheaper than the manpower and logistics spent fulfilling, and then refunding, orders nobody wanted.

OBSERVED VS PROPOSED

Same screen, one change: the button waits.

WHY THIS MATTERS NOW

An action this consequential needs a deliberate tap.

The UX principle

Placing an order is a real commitment: money changes hands, a delivery gets dispatched. On the Pay Online path, Zepto rightly asks users to confirm with a deliberate tap. A well-behaved interface never completes an action this consequential without one. Here, it does.

The regulatory landscape

India's Central Consumer Protection Authority named "Forced Action" as one of 13 official dark pattern categories in 2023, and in mid-2025 ordered e-commerce and quick-commerce platforms to self-audit for exactly this class of issue. Zepto was one of 26 platforms that self-declared itself free of dark patterns, then was fined ₹7 lakh by the CCPA in December 2025 for other checkout practices, including hidden charges and pre-checked add-ons. This particular flow wasn't part of that fine, but it lives in the same family of behavior regulators are actively watching.

The cost of trust

Quick commerce runs on habit: thin margins, daily use, and a customer's quiet trust that the app will do only what they asked it to do. A platform that's already been fined once for checkout practices can least afford a second pattern that makes users feel like something happened to them, not because of them.
